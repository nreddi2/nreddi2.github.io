Website Evaluations

CRAP stands for Contrast, Repetition, Alignment, and Proximity. These principles help explain why some websites feel easy to follow while others make finding information difficult.

I compared two homepages using the University of Wisconsin-Madison DesignLab's CRAP guide. My evaluations focus on how each page presents its content, rather than whether I like what it sells or offers. Reviewed October 21, 2026. The website links below open in new tabs.

Apple

Strong design: Apple's U.S. homepage introduces its products through large photos and short descriptions. Each product area gives visitors a clear next step, such as learning more or buying.

  • Contrast: Large product names stand out from smaller supporting text. Light and dark backgrounds separate the promotions, while blue buttons draw attention to the actions visitors can take.
  • Repetition: Product areas repeat the same pattern of a heading, short tagline, action buttons, and image. The consistent rounded buttons help me recognize where to click as I scroll.
  • Alignment: Product headings, descriptions, and buttons share a centered alignment above the photos. That common center line makes each promotion feel organized instead of scattered.
  • Proximity: Each product's name, tagline, and buttons sit close together. The space around these groups makes it easy to tell which actions belong to which product.

Arngren

Weak design: Arngren advertises vehicles, electronics, and gadgets. Its homepage displays so many competing listings that I find it difficult to decide where to start.

  • Contrast: Red prices, green headings, blue links, and bright highlights all compete for attention. Individual items stand out, but there is little hierarchy to tell me what matters most.
  • Repetition: Product photos and prices repeat, but their sizes and arrangements vary widely. Consistent product cards would make comparing listings much easier.
  • Alignment: Images and text start at uneven positions across the page instead of following a consistent grid. I have to keep shifting my attention to find the next product's details.
  • Proximity: Listings sit close to neighboring products with little empty space between them. Some labels stay near their photos, but the crowded layout makes the boundaries between products harder to follow.
